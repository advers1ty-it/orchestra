# Books: Leila Hormozi

Built from the 48 videos in sources.md (2021 to 2026). She has no published book of her own in these sources, solo or co-written; her leadership book is unpublished as of the newest source. This file covers that book in progress, the Acquisition.com material she teaches, and the books she cites as shaping how she leads and decides. Book text was not fetched. Summaries are in my own words, and Alex Hormozi's books and ideas are listed only where she teaches or cites them, not as her own work.

## Her own book in progress

### Her leadership book (unpublished; working title $100M Leadership in 2024)
- **Status:** In 2024 she mentioned an upcoming book called $100M Leadership (S031). In 2026 she said the first draft was done and she plans to self-publish in Q1 2027 because she did not like the publishing deals, with the supporting course and workbook given away free (S013). Earlier she described it as built from her lessons and failures (S005) and said writing it was hard because she dislikes working alone (S010).
- **Core thesis:** Leadership is the missing skill for founders who want money and status but not the responsibility of leading people, and existing advice is too vague to copy, so the book gives steps.
- **Key frameworks:** 12 steps and a flywheel built on a leadership cycle: lead yourself, lead others, lead leaders, create leaders, then create leaders who build a culture that creates leaders. Written for three readers: someone who has never led, someone with a handful of reports, and someone who wants to run a big team and develop leaders. "how to lead yourself, learning how to lead others, learning how to lead leaders, and then learning how to create leaders" (S013)
- **Taught in sources:** S005, S010, S013, S031. She also set aside an earlier book draft on changing your life because it was not ready (S013).

## Acquisition.com material she teaches

### The scaling roadmap (ten levels of business)
- **Core thesis:** Each stage of a company has one constraint and one function to build before it can move up.
- **Key frameworks:** Ten levels from improvise to institution, keyed to headcount (5 to 9, 10 to 19, 20 to 49, 50 to 99, 100 to 250, 250 to 500); the rule of 100 (100 lead-generation actions a day for 100 days, which she calls the Hormozi law); LTV to CAC fixes; manager, director, and executive roles; founder shifts from one-person army to architect. She notes she has not run a company above 500 people.
- **Taught in sources:** S019, where she says she built the roadmap with Alex. Stage thinking also in S005, S011, S015.

### Value Acceleration Method (VAM)
- **Core thesis:** Founders lack the who, the what, and the how to scale; Acquisition.com supplies all three to portfolio companies, starting with talent.
- **Key frameworks:** Who, what, how; talent acquisition as a mirrored funnel (applicant generation, nurture, scripted interview); a five-step interview with a CEO interview past 30 people.
- **Taught in sources:** S037, S038.

### Acquisition.com values
- **Core thesis:** Three values (sincere candor, unimpeachable character, competitive greatness) act as the company's decision filter for hiring, firing, deals, and recognition.
- **Key frameworks:** Values as a board of directors; values defined as behaviors; values before skill in hiring; shout-outs tied to a named value.
- **Taught in sources:** S005, S008, S014, S020, S022, S034, S038.

### Seven components of a sellable business
- **Core thesis:** Buyers buy a business, not a person, and want to see upside the founder has not taken.
- **Key frameworks:** Growth potential, unique value proposition, revenue diversification, cash flow, recurring revenue, financial performance, and team strength (the disappear-for-a-month test).
- **Taught in sources:** S027.

### Acquisition.com workshops
- **Core thesis:** Her team, not professional presenters, teaches the material, and teaching it is how they learn it.
- **Key frameworks:** Speaker training plans, live feedback and redo, edifying each presenter before they speak (she says presenter scores rose from about 80 to about 90).
- **Taught in sources:** S025, S026, S033.

### Gym Launch Secrets (an Alex Hormozi book)
- **Core thesis:** As she cites it, retention for gym owners.
- **Key frameworks:** The five horsemen of retention, which she names when answering a retention question.
- **Taught in sources:** S030 only, in passing.

## Books she cites

### John Wooden's Pyramid of Success and his leadership book (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Success is peace of mind from doing your best, built from traits stacked in a pyramid; great leaders are first teachers.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Competitive greatness as a company value; a CEO self-rating exercise on each pyramid trait, which she says she has run with about 900 CEOs; industriousness and enthusiasm as the base; the coach who stays off the court.
- **Taught in sources:** S003, S014, S029, S031, S036, S041.

### The Hard Thing About Hard Things (Ben Horowitz, 2014)
- **Core thesis:** Running a company is mostly handling hard situations with no clean answer.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Peacetime versus wartime CEO modes, applied to which people fit which season of a business. She still recommends it to her team.
- **Taught in sources:** S015, S041, S043.

### Patrick Lencioni's books
- **Core thesis:** Short, easy reads on teams and leadership.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. She says teams should read all of them, one a month, and that the right book is the one for the weakness you have now.
- **Taught in sources:** S031, S043.

### Jim Collins's books
- **Core thesis:** Researched distillations of what the best companies do.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** The flywheel, which she uses to decide whether a new division makes the others better.
- **Taught in sources:** S026, S031.

### Pour Your Heart Into It (Howard Schultz, 1997)
- **Core thesis:** The Starbucks turnaround came from many small moves, not one big one.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Many small fixes over one strategy change; listen to the front line.
- **Taught in sources:** S041.

### John Mackey's book on Whole Foods (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Make a decision only when it is a win for customers, employees, community, and investors.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Win win win: she asks how the team wins first so the business wins.
- **Taught in sources:** S002.

### Matt Mochary's CEO book
- **Core thesis:** A tactical manual for running a company.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. She calls it The Tactical Guide to Being a CEO, says she loved it, and asked the author to mentor her. (His published book is The Great CEO Within, subtitled The Tactical Guide to Company Building.)
- **Taught in sources:** S031.

### John Maxwell (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Leadership is caught, not taught.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Demonstrate before you duplicate.
- **Taught in sources:** S028.

### A book by the CEO of Blackstone (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** As she reads it, a big company is as easy to build as a small one.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Top talent follows a big vision.
- **Taught in sources:** S003.

### Albert Ellis and Steven Hayes (behavioral psychology)
- **Core thesis:** You can change how you act regardless of why you started acting that way, and thoughts are sentences, not commands.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Act on behavior, not feeling; judge a thought by whether it is useful, not whether it is true; process over emotion when deciding. She names Ellis's How to Control Your Anxiety Before It Controls You and rereads the psychology books yearly.
- **Taught in sources:** S006, S010, S014; the unnamed behavioral psychology books also in S002 and S009.

### The Comfort Crisis (Michael Easter, 2021)
- **Core thesis:** Modern comfort makes people weaker, so seek discomfort on purpose.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Comfort creep and stair-stepping toward discomfort.
- **Taught in sources:** S004.

### Early reading: Rich Dad Poor Dad (Robert Kiyosaki, 1997), Tony Robbins, and Jim Rohn
- **Core thesis:** Money is a learnable skill, and early in a career you work to learn, not to earn.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Work to learn when young; learn sales first.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S005, S008.

### Books on meetings (titles not spoken)
- **Core thesis:** She read six or seven books on meetings after realizing she ran bad ones, then cut them to what is useful.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Pulse, purpose, and preparation for meeting cadence.
- **Taught in sources:** S040.

### Ideas she quotes without walking through a book
- Warren Buffett on avoiding dumb decisions (S044), Charlie Munger on incentives and the Peter Principle (S026), Russell Brunson's mass movement formula for community (S038), and Zig Ziglar on building people (S037).
