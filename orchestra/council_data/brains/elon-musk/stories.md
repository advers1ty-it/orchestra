# Stories: Elon Musk

Built from the seven batch notes over 39 sources (2012 to 2026). 20 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Starship deletes: legs, door, thrusters, and Raptor parts.** The booster lost its landing legs so the tower could catch it, work on the payload door stopped because early ships carry no satellites, stage separation now uses the main engines and vented gas instead of a cold gas thruster system, and each Raptor version sheds plumbing until "it looks like it's got parts missing" (S012). Lesson: move mass to the ground, punt what is off the critical path, and the best part is no part (S006, S009, S012, S014, S038).
2. **Twitter takeover and cuts.** He describes arriving to a nosedive: about $1.5 billion of new debt service, negative cash flow, and staff cut from about 7,800 to 1,500, some of them wrongly. The headquarters had about 5% occupancy, a cafeteria where "there were more people making the food than eating the food." (S019), and supply contracts for empty buildings. Lesson: in a crisis cut fast and accept errors, and audit spend that nobody owns (S029, S031, S019, S012, S017).
3. **OpenAI as the counterweight that turned.** Larry Page called him a speciesist, Google held most AI talent and compute, so he funded and named OpenAI as an open source nonprofit and recruited Ilya Sutskever as the linchpin. It became closed and for profit, and on not securing governance he says "I fully admit to being a huge idiot here." (S029) Lesson: money without governance buys no control; he now builds his own lab and participates (S001, S019, S029, S026, S013).
4. **SpaceX's fourth Falcon 1 launch, 2008.** He expected under a 10% chance of success, messed up the first three launches, and the fourth was the last one the money could pay for; a NASA resupply contract followed. "if the fourth launch had failed, SpaceX would be dead in two thousand and eight." (S028) Lesson: write down the odds, keep the chips on the table, and know the survival margin is thin (S021, S028, S037, S003).
5. **Tesla's Christmas Eve round, 2008.** The financing closed at 6 PM on Christmas Eve, the last hour possible, with payroll due to bounce days later. He put in all the money he had left and slept in a friend's spare room. Lesson: invest everything only when the alternative is that the companies die, and hold reserves the rest of the time (S017, S023, S028, S021).
6. **Model 3 production hell.** He lived in the Fremont and Nevada factories for about three years and slept on the floor so the team could see he shared the pain, while Tesla sat on "the ragged edge of bankruptcy" (S017). He admits they messed up almost every part of the line. Lesson: prototypes are easy, volume production without going bankrupt is the hard part, and the leader belongs on the floor (S024, S014, S034, S017).
7. **Colossus in months, not two years.** Suppliers quoted 18 to 24 months for a 100,000-GPU cluster. He broke it into building, power, cooling, power smoothing, and networking, took an existing factory, rented generators and about a quarter of US mobile cooling, added Megapacks for power swings, and did cabling himself in round-the-clock shifts. Colossus-2 later needed self-designed transformers. Lesson: decompose an "impossible" quote into parts and attack each (S021, S035, S002).
8. **The Space Shuttle that would not change.** Known O-ring and foam issues were tolerated because the vehicle had flown before, the heat shield took about nine months to refurbish, and it cost more per ton than an expendable rocket. Lesson: punishing change kills iteration, and reuse only counts if it is rapid and complete (S009, S011, S018, S025).
9. **Nvidia said no, so Tesla built a chip team.** Nvidia would not build a cheap, power-efficient car computer, so he hired Jim Keller and built the chip and AI teams from scratch; Terafab follows the same logic for wafers and memory. Lesson: when the best case from every supplier is not enough, build the capacity yourself, starting small (S033, S039, S018, S005).
10. **Tesla's two dumb ideas: the Lotus Elise and AC Propulsion.** The plan was to license a drivetrain and modify a Lotus chassis cheaply. In the end about 7% of Lotus parts carried over, the car was much heavier, and none of the AC Propulsion technology was used. Lesson: question the premise early, and knock the house down rather than modify it (S008, S023).
11. **Bill Gates and the electric Semi.** Gates said a long-range electric semi could not work. Musk asked which battery or efficiency number he thought was wrong, and Gates knew neither. Lesson: put numbers on any claim and argue the numbers (S019, S008).
12. **Zip2's hired CEO and media board.** After VC funding, investors installed a professional CEO and media-company customers sat on the board, pushing the software toward uses that made no sense. Lesson: do not cede control to people whose interests pull the product elsewhere, and go direct to consumers (S003, S021).
13. **Not starting Tesla with JB Straubel.** He meant to start an EV company with JB, joined the Eberhard group instead, tried not to be CEO, and had to become CEO anyway. He calls it his worst business decision. Lesson: pick the partner you trust, and if you care about the company you will end up running it (S017, S024).
14. **Model 3 battery line: five steps done backwards.** A robot cell choked output. He tried to automate it, then speed it up, then simplify the glue path, and only then asked what the fiberglass mats were for. Each team pointed to another, a microphone test showed no difference, and deleting the mats bypassed a $2 million robot cell. Lesson: question the requirement and delete before you optimize (S014).
15. **Steel instead of carbon fiber.** Carbon fiber Starship progress was slow (an autoclave bigger than the rocket, wrinkles). Stainless steel is similar in strength to weight at cryogenic temperatures, costs about 50 times less in raw material, and is easy to work. "It was dumb not to do steel." (S002) Lesson: when progress is slow, change the material, not the schedule (S002).
16. **Falcon 1's hypergolic upper stage.** The first spreadsheet in Tom Mueller's kitchen had an upper stage on expensive, toxic propellants, and no amount of genius could make it affordable, so it was dropped. Lesson: do not optimize a thing that should not exist (S014).
17. **Russian ICBMs and the raw-material math.** In 2001 and 2002 he tried to buy ICBMs for a Mars mission, the price kept rising, and he found a rocket's raw materials were only 1 to 2% of its historical cost. Lesson: the blocker was cost, not will, so attack cost from first principles (S021).
18. **Giga casting from toys.** He took the idea of casting from toy making and asked six casting machine makers; five said no, one said maybe, and "I'll take that as a yes." (S017) About 120 body pieces became one. Lesson: ask whether physics forbids it, then find the one supplier who will try (S017).
19. **The Starlink team in 2018.** Weekly reviews showed the Redmond team's progress was not converging, so he concluded success was not among the possible outcomes and took drastic action. Lesson: plot each review as a data point, and act hard only when success is out of the set (S002).
20. **The intern's requirement.** A requirement credited to the aero loads department turned out to come from an intern who had left two years earlier, and nobody in the department agreed with it. He says this has happened several times. Lesson: every requirement needs a named owner, not a department (S014).
