# Books: Naval Ravikant

Built from the eight extract batches over 35 sources (2009 to 2026). Summaries are in my own words; no book text was fetched.

Naval has written no books of his own. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (2020) was compiled and edited by Eric Jorgenson from his tweets, podcasts, and interviews; it is not in the corpus, and its material lives in the sources below. In 2026 he gives his reasons for not writing one: partly laziness, and "haven't written a book is because I feel like it might be fraudulent" (S032), since he does not consider himself enlightened. His thinking lives in the essay and podcast series below, then in the books he recommends.

## His essays and podcast series

### How to Get Rich (tweetstorm and podcast series; transcript dated 2019-12-28)
- **Core thesis:** Wealth comes from owning assets that earn while you sleep, built by applying specific knowledge with accountability and leverage, steered by judgment. You will not get rich renting out your time.
- **Key frameworks:** wealth versus money versus status; specific knowledge, accountability, leverage, judgment; labor, capital, and code and media as leverage; permissionless leverage; inputs versus outputs; aspirational hourly rate; long-term games with long-term people; productize yourself; escape competition through authenticity; avoid ruin. He later says the title was deliberate clickbait and should have been about creating wealth (S001, S030).
- **Taught in sources:** S001 (the full series), with episode cuts in S004 and S005; restated in S017, S029, S030, S032, and S034.

### How to Angel Invest, Parts 1 and 2 (2019-11-17 and 2020-06-12)
- **Core thesis:** Early-stage returns follow a power law, so access to the few winners and judgment about founders matter more than anything else; equity beats wages.
- **Key frameworks:** capital, judgment, dealflow; dealflow versus access; access comes from an authentic brand; power-law portfolio math; non-consensus and right; weird deals with one fatal flaw; first-time versus repeat founders; integrity over niceness; a 24-hour cooling-off period.
- **Taught in sources:** S024, S025.

### Early nav.al essays (2009 to 2011)
- **Core thesis:** The internet cut the cost of starting a company so far that leverage, not talent, explains the speed of new fortunes, and the funding industry is restructuring around that.
- **Key frameworks:** returns to entrepreneurship come from leverage; early hires as late founders; investors herd rationally (a Keynesian beauty contest); funding markets develop in reverse; venture capital unbundled into advice, control, and money; respect the other person's time when pitching.
- **Taught in sources:** S018, S019, S020, S021, S022, S023.

### Naval Podcast short episodes (2019 to 2021)
- **Core thesis:** Short takes on compounding, building and selling, feedback from reality, and knowledge as the real resource.
- **Key frameworks:** long-term games with long-term people; learn to sell, learn to build; groups never admit failure, so prefer for-profits that face reality; knowledge makes resources effectively infinite; credibility moving from institutions to individuals.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S005, S013, S014, S015, S026.

### Naval Podcast, AI and building series (2025 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** AI is a motorcycle for the mind that hands huge leverage to people with taste, judgment, and agency. Small teams of geniuses can now build what once took companies, and pure software is no longer a moat.
- **Key frameworks:** curate people; the four things a founder cannot delegate; minimum-sized groups; good products are hard to vary; life in the arena and learning loops; waste tokens, save time; always the smartest model; humans as verifiers; pure software is uninvestable; sell the truth and walk from bad deals.
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S003, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S028, S029, S035 (S027 is a hiring notice with no content of his).

### Long interviews (2016 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** The same ideas in conversation, plus how he decides, spends time, and lives.
- **Key frameworks:** decision heuristics (if you cannot decide, no); explore then exploit; iterations, not hours; asymmetric bets with a wide funnel; three ways to retire; desire as a contract to be unhappy; judgment ladder to taste.
- **Taught in sources:** Tim Ferriss (S016, 2016), Joe Rogan (S017, 2019), The Knowledge Project (S031, 2019), BeerBiceps (S033, 2024), Modern Wisdom (S030, 2025), Internapalooza with Cory Levy (S034, 2025), and Eric Jorgenson's megasode (S032, 2026).

## Books he recommends (business and decisions first)

- **The Beginning of Infinity (David Deutsch, 2011):** Good explanations are hard to vary, and knowledge, not raw resources, is the source of wealth and progress. He takes from it his later definition of wealth, the hard-to-vary test for products, and conjecture and criticism as the learning loop. He calls the first three and last few chapters the accessible ones. (S028, S029, S032; also S014, S015)
- **Skin in the Game (Nassim Taleb, 2018):** Those who take risk should bear it, and systems stay honest only when they do. He calls it required reading and rereads it; it underpins his accountability and ruin rules. (S001, S032) He also borrows Taleb's IYI label from the Incerto and says Antifragile does not stick for him for lack of practical examples (S030, S032); The Bed of Procrustes is recommended as aphorisms (S016).
- **Influence (Robert Cialdini, 1984):** The six levers of persuasion. He calls it his only sales training besides Glengarry Glen Ross, uses the checklist rarely, and says the sequel Pre-Suasion is not needed except for anchoring. (S011, S031)
- **The Intelligent Investor (Benjamin Graham, 1949):** Cited through Buffett's apprenticeship with Graham as the model for paying to learn from the best. (S001)
- **The Strategy of Conflict (Thomas Schelling, 1960):** Source of Schelling points, which he uses for coordination, missions, and credibility filters. (S001, S026, S032)
- **How to Get Rich (Felix Dennis, 2006):** A favorite when young; credible because Dennis made his money elsewhere. (S001)
- **How to be an Angel Investor (Paul Graham, essay) and The High Growth Handbook (Elad Gil, 2018):** The first for angel basics; the second as an example of an investor brand built through content. (S024)
- **The Macintosh Way (Guy Kawasaki, 1990), Liftoff (Eric Berger, 2021), and The Soul of a New Machine (Tracy Kidder, 1981):** Small elite teams building something great; The Macintosh Way pulled him to Silicon Valley. (S011, S033)
- **Wind, Sand and Stars and Airman's Odyssey (Antoine de Saint-Exupery):** Lead by making people yearn for the mission rather than issuing orders. (S011)
- **The Elon Musk book by Eric Jorgenson:** Inspiring on agency and urgency, not a process to copy. (S029)
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack (Charlie Munger) and Farnam Street:** Places to collect mental models, though the hard part is knowing which to use. He does not run checklists, and says The Checklist Manifesto suits pilots and surgeons, not investors. (S001, S031)
- **Matt Ridley (The Rational Optimist and the rest):** He recommends everything Ridley wrote; it feeds his positive-sum, rational-optimist view. (S016)
- **Original sources over summaries:** The Wealth of Nations, Origin of Species, and Feynman's Six Easy Pieces, plus Thing Explainer and Thinking Physics for explaining from first principles. (S001, S031)
- **History:** The Lessons of History (Will and Ariel Durant) is a short book he rereads instead of the full Story of Civilization. (S016, S029, S031, S032)
- **Books he argues against or reads with care:** Outliers, because mastery is 10,000 iterations, not hours (S030, S032); Superintelligence, read with the rebuttals (S031, a 2019 view); Ricardo's comparative advantage, which he says ignores scale economies and network effects (S035); Girard's mimetic desire, via Thiel, which he accepts (S030).
- **Off-topic for this seat (philosophy and inner life), listed for completeness:** Schopenhauer's essays, Meditations, Seneca's letters, Krishnamurti's Total Freedom, Daniel Ingram's Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha, the Ashtavakra Gita, Jed McKenna, The Prophet, William Glasser, The Power of Habit, and his brother Kamal Ravikant's book. (S016, S029, S030, S032, S033)
