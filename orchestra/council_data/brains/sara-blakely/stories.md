# Stories: Sara Blakely

Built from the batch 01 to 05 notes over 33 sources. 20 stories and cases she reuses, with the lesson she draws. Source ids in parentheses; most-told first. Where her numbers or details drift between retellings, the entry says so.

1. **The wrong movie.** Seven years into selling fax machines door to door, she pulled off the road after a bad day, wrote that sales was her one strength, asked why, and wrote the goal of inventing a product she could sell to millions. The idea took two years to arrive. "I'm in the wrong movie like call the director call the writer cut this is not my life" (S007) Lesson: decide the outcome and the why before you know the how (S001, S002, S004, S007, S008, S009, S016, S018, S021, S023, S025, S033).

2. **The Neiman Marcus bathroom pitch.** She found the buyer's number, kept calling without leaving a message, flew to Dallas with the prototype in a Ziploc bag and her lucky red backpack, and when she felt the buyer drifting took her to the restroom for a live before and after. The buyer ordered for seven stores. "In the middle of my meeting with her, I could tell I was losing her." (S032) Lesson: when a product is best proven live, go in person and show it (S001, S002, S006, S007, S017, S018, S019, S023, S024, S030, S032).

3. **The mill owner's daughters.** Every North Carolina hosiery mill, all run by men, said no by phone and then in person, and each asked who she was, who she was with, and who backed her; she answered with her own name. Weeks later one owner called back because his daughters liked the idea. Lesson: the yes came from a person with a stake in the problem, and her enthusiasm stuck. The detail drifts: three daughters (S001, S002) or two (S014, S019) (S001, S002, S007, S008, S014, S017, S018, S019, S020).

4. **Writing her own patent.** Attorneys quoted more than her savings, so she bought a patents and trademarks book at Barnes & Noble, wrote it herself, had her mother draw the sketch, and paid a lawyer only for the claims. He later admitted he thought she was a hidden-camera prank, and a misheard "lacquer" for Lycra nearly went into the filing. Lesson: do the legwork, buy only the expert piece, and check details before you submit. The quotes drift between tellings: $2,000 to $5,000 (S014), $3,000 to $5,000 (S019), $5,000 to $10,000 with a $750 claims fee (S007) (S002, S007, S008, S014, S017, S018, S019, S025).

5. **Paying friends to buy it.** After the Neiman order she called friends in all seven cities, mailed them checks, and gave them a script to ask for Spanx at the counter so it would sell through and earn a reorder. "I've got to have these things move off the shelf or the buyer's not going to give me any more chance" (S032) Lesson: with no ad money, manufacture the first demand yourself (S002, S007, S017, S018, S019, S024, S032).

6. **Oprah and the fake staff meeting.** She mailed Oprah a gift basket; Oprah's stylist put it in her wardrobe and it became a favorite thing. When the crew asked to film headquarters and a staff meeting, she had friends, including Connie from the mailbox store, sit on her apartment floor. "Connie can you be at my house and look like you work for me" (S014) Lesson: say yes first and build the thing after (S001, S002, S008, S014, S017, S018, S019, S020, S027).

7. **Spanx days: a sales force off the payroll.** For about two years she worked department-store floors: a morning rally for all staff, free product for the makeup, shoe, and couture teams with each told what was in it for them, a laminated before-and-after of her own rear, and all-day selling. "I inadvertently was building a Salesforce that wasn't on my payroll" (S017) Lesson: the order is the start; win the floor yourself (S002, S007, S009, S011, S017, S021, S023).

8. **Breaking store rules.** To get Spanx out of the sleepy hosiery corner she put product by the registers, once duct-taping a bin to a register and getting caught on store video. The Neiman CEO told staff to let her keep going. "I started getting away with it cuz everybody thought somebody else approved" (S017) Lesson: put the product where the buyer actually shops, and ask forgiveness later (S007, S017).

9. **No crotches.** Right after landing Neiman she learned the mill had no free machines for gussets, searched the Yellow Pages, and found a supplier near Atlanta. "Okay, let me get this straight. I just landed Neiman Marcus and I have no crotches." (S019) Lesson: land the order, then solve supply fast; unknowns get solved by doing (S001, S002, S017, S018, S019, S025).

10. **Naming Spanx in the car.** She wanted the hard K sound she saw in Kodak and Coca-Cola and that comedians say gets laughs, swapped the letters for an X because invented words recall and trademark better, and registered it online that night. Some stores refused the name as offensive. Lesson: a name people repeat is free marketing, even with some lost doors. The trademark cost drifts: $300 (S019) or $150 (S025) (S001, S002, S008, S014, S018, S019, S025, S032).

11. **The $18 website.** Her first site was a scan of the packaging and a buy button. "my entire website cost me $18 a month for two years and it was just the color copy of the packaging and then a buy button" (S001) Lesson: build the minimum thing that takes orders. Duration drifts: two years (S001) or a year and a half (S017) (S001, S017).

12. **QVC on her price.** People warned QVC would kill a Neiman brand and that everyone discounts there; she went on without discounting, after a 2001 Forbes article moved a stuck buyer. She sold thousands of pairs in minutes versus dozens a day in a store. Lesson: TV shopping is a free ad if you hold your price. Numbers drift: 3,000 to 9,000 in eight minutes (S014), about 20,000 in eight minutes (S007), 10,000 in six minutes (S006) (S006, S007, S014, S022).

13. **Knockoffs paid for her awareness.** Big companies copied Spanx early and spent heavily on ads; buyers tried the inferior versions and came back. "i had big companies knock me off very early on and they spent eight to twenty million dollars on ad budgets that gave awareness for the product that i had just created" (S021) Lesson: a better product can ride a rival's ad budget, and a patent is only worth the fight you will pay for (S014, S021, S023).

14. **Business is war.** At a party in her late twenties two men told her to get ready because business is war; she went home and decided to build with intuition, humor, and kindness instead. Lesson: reject the default script for how a company must be run (S002, S003, S007, S012, S016, S034).

15. **The men's undershirt.** She saw the men's undershirt as unchanged for about a century and remade it with some Lycra and a tapered waist. "we reinvented the man's undershirt because it had been ignored for a hundred years" (S024) Lesson: look for products nobody has questioned in decades (S001, S002, S016, S024).

16. **Oops meetings.** Spanx staff, often Sara first, stood up to share a mistake with a theme song while the company danced. Her own examples: selling too much to wholesale accounts and forgetting the end consumer, and staying in shapewear too long. Lesson: make failure safe and public, then act on it (S018, S021).

17. **Selling in empty stores after 9/11, and the 2020 shutdown.** Near launch, after 9/11, she stood in empty department stores all day and built deep ties with associates who then sold for her. In 2020 she ran a set order: people, cost cuts by zero-based budget, inventory renegotiation, online sales, a three-week plan. Lesson: in a crisis, protect people and cash and keep selling (S011, S015, S033).

18. **The wrong leader.** In some stretches she drifted from product while someone else ran the company, felt burned out, and later stepped back in. "I stopped honoring my lane in the business and I had the wrong leader in the business" (S007) Lesson: the founder's lane is product and brand; guard it (S007, S016, S020).

19. **Sneex: ten years and a sneaker cut in half.** Sneex started from her own wish for a comfortable heel and a designer's TV remark that comfort would be an insult, which she took as her sign. Factories fired her after six to eight months; four years in, the heel was not 10x better, so she pulled the plug, cut a sneaker and a stiletto in half, glued them, and walked in them at home. She then held the finished stock a year before launching when her small team said they believed in it. Lesson: let the goal reshape the product, and launch only past your own bar (S003, S004, S006, S009, S020, S022, S028).

20. **Selling the majority of Spanx in 2021.** After 21 years as sole owner she sold a majority stake, kept a large piece and the chair, and says the reason was freeing her mental share, not money. "the core reason why I sold was not for money. Spanx was very profitable." (S004) Lesson: a sale is a decision about control and attention; be ready to let go of the outcome (S003, S004, S005, S021, S025).
